Kin

किन

Eleven years old, born in a gali off Chawri Bazar, and the fastest way through Purani Dilli that is not on any map.

Kin flies kites from the rooftops, knows which shutter opens first and which chaiwala gives credit, and will walk you to Jama Masjid by a route you could never repeat. Cheeky, quick, warm. Talks with the whole body.

Shape
Big square-round head, small body, long limbs. A kite diamond on the back makes the silhouette.
Carries
A patang, a charkhi of thread, one loose chappal strap.
Tell
One tuft of hair that never lies flat. The gamcha tail arrives a beat after Kin does.

How Kin moves

  1. Eyes, head, body. The eyes go first, the head follows two frames later, the body last.
  2. Wind up. A dip the opposite way before every run, jump and point.
  3. Squash on landing, stretch on launch, volume kept, held for three frames at most.
  4. The gamcha is late. Scarf tails and the hair tuft trail every move and overshoot the stop.
  5. Never straight. Hands and kite travel on arcs.
  6. Hold the pose. Kin hits a clear silhouette and stays in it long enough to read.
  7. Print look. One ink weight, flat colour set slightly off register, halftone only on the shadow side.
  8. Never mean. Kin teases the visitor, never the street or its people.
