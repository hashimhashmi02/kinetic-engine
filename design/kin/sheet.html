Kin

किन

Eleven years old, born in a gali off Chawri Bazar, and the fastest way through Purani Dilli that is not on any map.

Kin flies kites from the rooftops, knows which shutter opens first and which chaiwala gives credit, and will walk you to Jama Masjid by a route you could never repeat. Quick and cheeky in the lanes, quiet on the rooftops at dusk, when the kites go up.

Shape
Big soft head, small body in a loose slate-blue kurta, white and grey scarf, thin limbs. A red kite on the back makes the silhouette.
Carries
A patang, a charkhi of thread, one loose chappal strap.
Tell
One tuft of hair that never lies flat. The scarf tail arrives a beat after Kin does.

How Kin moves

  1. Eyes, head, body. The eyes go first, the head follows two frames later, the body last.
  2. Wind up. A dip the opposite way before every run, jump and point.
  3. Squash on landing, stretch on launch, volume kept, held for three frames at most.
  4. The scarf is late. Scarf tails and the hair tuft trail every move and overshoot the stop.
  5. Never straight. Hands and kite travel on arcs.
  6. Hold the pose. Kin hits a clear silhouette and stays in it long enough to read.
  7. Cut paper. No outlines. Every part is its own sheet with grain, and edges come from the shadow it casts on the sheet below.
  8. One red. The kite is the only saturated colour in any frame. The white scarf is the brightest value; the eye finds Kin by the two.
  9. Never mean. Kin teases the visitor, never the street or its people.
